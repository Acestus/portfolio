Oxide's Operations Software Engineer role sits at an intersection I know well: supporting colleagues, improving operational systems, and making repeatable work easier to trust. My background is in cloud infrastructure and operations, with hands-on work in automation, identity, monitoring, networking, and delivery pipelines. That experience gives me a practical foundation for building software that helps internal teams do their work well.

The role calls for systems that connect company data, automate manual operations, support internal services, and improve resilience. Those are not abstract ideas to me. They are recurring problems in infrastructure work: information is spread across tools, routine tasks consume attention, and a change is only useful when people can understand and operate it.

## I build automation around real operational work

In my current Cloud Infrastructure Engineer role, I write infrastructure-as-code and CI/CD pipelines, support Kubernetes and cloud applications, work on cost management and analytic relational databases, and create automation scripts with technical documentation and videos. Earlier, as a Microsoft Cloud Engineer, I created PowerShell runbooks for Azure Automation and Logic Apps, helped deploy monitoring, queried operational data with Kusto, and responded to security events.

That experience maps directly to Oxide's interest in replacing manual operations with useful internal tools. Good automation starts with understanding the operator's actual steps, the failure cases, and the evidence needed to know whether the task finished. It should reduce toil without hiding the state of the system.

## I bring an operations and data perspective

Oxide describes internal systems that combine data from external stores with company information, including data pipelines across product and manufacturing work. My experience includes analytic relational databases, Azure Monitor and Log Analytics queries, Resource Graph, and telemetry collection through agents and data collection rules. I have worked with operational data to help teams investigate systems, not just to produce a dashboard.

I would bring that habit of asking what question the data should answer, where the source of truth lives, who owns the result, and how freshness or missing data affects a decision. My resume does not claim direct Salesforce, Zendesk, or NetSuite integration work, so I would approach those systems as APIs to learn and integrate carefully rather than imply experience I have not had.

## Security and reliability belong in the workflow

Across Azure architecture and infrastructure roles, I have worked with Entra identity, MFA, PIM and RBAC, network security, Sentinel, secure-score improvements, and FedRAMP coordination. I have deployed and supported cloud services, networks, and Kubernetes workloads, and built release workflows with Bicep and CI/CD.

Those experiences shape how I think about internal software: make access explicit, keep permissions scoped, expose useful operational status, and include recovery and support in the design. I have written about these patterns in the [portfolio delivery pipeline](/articles/github-actions-portfolio/), [Azure Functions release design](/articles/azure-functions-python-cicd/), and [VM patch management](/articles/azure-vm-patch-management-as-code/). These are public examples of my engineering approach; they should not be read as claims that a particular customer environment passed an audit.

## Documentation is part of the system

I have created runbooks, technical documentation, and videos alongside the automation itself. That matters in a role supporting colleagues across internal services and SaaS tools. A system is easier to operate when its purpose, permissions, failure behavior, and recovery steps are written down where the team can find them.

My working style is to make changes reviewable, keep examples reproducible, and explain what a check proves. The [Bicep and GitHub Actions guide](/docs/bicep-github-actions-trunk/) and the [operations assistant design](/articles/purpose-built-ai-a-field-toolkit-for-engineering-operations/) show how I apply those ideas in public work.

## A direct note on the software stack

Oxide names Rust and TypeScript in the role. My professional experience is strongest in cloud infrastructure, Python, PowerShell, Linux operations, and automation; I do not present my resume as evidence of production Rust or TypeScript service ownership. My portfolio includes a small Rust and WebAssembly particle simulation, and I continue to expand my software engineering practice. I would bring an honest account of that experience, a strong operational foundation, and a willingness to learn Oxide's codebase and conventions.

The part of the role that most motivates me is its focus on helping every team work more effectively. I have spent my career close to the systems people depend on and the operational tasks they need to complete. At Oxide, I could apply that perspective to internal tools, data flows, and automation that make colleagues' work clearer, safer, and more dependable.
